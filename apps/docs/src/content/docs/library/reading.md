## Work with a passage

Passages use the full width of the page, on a book's page and in Highlights.
Point at a passage (or move to it with <kbd>Tab</kbd>) and a small row of
buttons appears at its right:

- **Add to thematic thread**, to follow its idea alongside passages from
  other books.
- **Manage tags**, to tag this passage on its own.
- **Add another note** beside it.
- **Edit quote**, to correct the text.
- **More passage actions** (…): see the imported original, or delete the
  passage.

If you've turned on [AI](/ai/gemini/), **Explain this passage** is there too.
On a book's page, passages keep loading as you scroll, so long books open
quickly. A passage you reach from a link, such as Today's highlight or a
search result, is marked so your eye lands on it.

## Organise

- **Tags** go on books and on individual passages.
- **Collections** gather books, such as "Book club" or "Re-read".
- **Threads** follow an idea across books: add a passage to a thread with
  **Add to thematic thread**, then open **Threads** to read them together.
- **Reading status** is unread, reading, paused, or finished. Each change is kept, so a book's page shows its reading history.

### Reading a thread

The **Threads** page opens with a few numbers: how many threads you have,
how many passages are in them, how many books they connect, and your largest
thread. Below, **Your threads** lists each thread with its passage and book
counts and when you last added to it. Choose one to read it beside the list:
its description, the books it draws from, and every passage with its book and
date. On each passage, the arrow opens it in its book, and **×** takes it out
of the thread (the passage stays in your library). **Edit** renames a thread or
changes its description; the bin deletes the thread, never its passages.

## Look up a word offline

The desktop app includes an [offline English dictionary](/library/dictionary/)
based on WordNet 3.1. Select
one word in a passage and choose **Define**, or open **Offline dictionary**
(the book icon in the header) to search for a word or short phrase.

Definitions, parts of speech, and synonyms are bundled with the app. Lookup
works without an account, AI key, or internet connection, and sends no text
away. If a word has no entry, try its singular or base form. Press
<kbd>Escape</kbd> to close the dictionary and return to reading.

## Get passages back

- **Search** sits at the top of every page. Press <kbd>⌘</kbd>+<kbd>K</kbd>
  (<kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>K</kbd> on Linux) and type a word you remember: the
  best passages and notes show as you type, and the last row opens Highlights
  with every one that contains it.
- **Highlights** lists every passage and note, with filters.
- **The library page** opens with an overview of your reading: how many
  passages and notes you've kept, your busiest day and longest run of days,
  a calendar of when you highlighted, and the books you highlighted most.
- **Today's highlight** on the library page shows one passage each day and
  gives every book a fair turn. **Another passage** picks a different one.
- **Statistics** shows a year of highlighting day by day, which months and
  weekdays you highlight most, how long your passages are, which books and
  authors you've highlighted most, and how many books you finished each year.
  It's worked out from your library; nothing extra is tracked.
- **What these numbers use** on the same page says how much of your library
  each chart can draw on. E-readers don't record when you finished a book, so
  "Books finished each year" counts only books with a finish date, which you
  can add on the book's page.
